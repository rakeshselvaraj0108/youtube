## Scope

Content that equips a viewer to deceive, defraud or gain unauthorised access: account compromise, circumvention of payment, forged documents, academic dishonesty and trespass.

## Fully monetized when

- Educational or humorous reference without instruction
- Journalistic reporting on fraud or its victims

## Limited ads when

- Demonstration of a circumvention technique without a complete method

## No ads when

- Step-by-step instructions enabling unauthorised access or fraud
- Promotion of essay mills or academic cheating services
- Glorification of trespass

## Documented exemptions

- Educational, documentary, scientific or artistic (EDSA) framing where the context is evident from the content itself rather than asserted in metadata
- News reporting on a matter of public interest
- Non-graphic verbal reference without depiction
- Clearly fictional or scripted context, signposted as such
- Quotation of a third party, particularly where it is also condemned
- Security research with a defensive framing, including authorised penetration testing and bug-bounty work
- Consumer-protection content teaching recognition of a scam

## Signals that distinguish this clause from neighbours

- vs AF-05 (harmful acts): AF-11 risks property, money or access. AF-05 risks a body.
- The defensive framing exemption is narrow: recognising a scam is exempt, reproducing it end to end is not.

## Remediation guidance

- Preferred fix: MUTE
- Typical span: Often the whole instructional passage. CUT may be warranted.
